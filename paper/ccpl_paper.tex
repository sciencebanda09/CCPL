\begin{abstract}
Safety costs in constrained reinforcement learning are often observed after
the action that initiated them. A learner that assigns the emitted cost to the
latest action can update the wrong part of the policy, especially when a risk
pipeline contains several intervening actions. We present Causal
Consequence-Penalized Learning (CCPL), an implementation for this setting. It
combines delay-aware consequence targets, explicit replay alignment, an
action-centred Interventional Consequence Net (ICN), state-conditioned safety
weighting, uncertainty margins, and separate reward and consequence critics.
The ICN is trained with labels generated by a specified simulator structural
causal model (SCM); consequently, our experiments evaluate attribution under
known causal supervision and do not claim causal discovery from observational
data. In an archived six-environment, three-seed benchmark, CCPL obtains the
best reported reward--consequence trade-off in the supplied snapshot. A
matched ablation and a SafeRoute case study illustrate the mechanisms. We
separate these empirical findings from formal guarantees: the paper derives
an implementation-aligned fixed-kernel contraction calculation, but makes no
claim of convergence for the full neural training procedure or universal
safety in deployment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Constrained reinforcement learning (RL) seeks a policy that maximizes utility
while limiting an expected safety consequence. The standard CMDP formulation
is useful, but its training signal is frequently treated as if the observed
cost and the most recent action were causally paired. That shortcut is
reasonable for an immediate cost. It is not reasonable when a consequence
passes through a delayed physical or operational pipeline.

Suppose an action at time $t$ initiates a process and its consequence is
observed at $t+\tau_t$. The learner sees the consequence at the latter time,
after actions $a_{t+1},\ldots,a_{t+\tau_t-1}$ have already occurred. Assigning
the observation to $a_{t+\tau_t}$ is a temporal attribution error. It can
produce a policy that avoids actions correlated with a dangerous state while
failing to correct the action that changes the eventual consequence.

CCPL makes this failure mode explicit. Its central design is a controlled
learning framework with five connected pieces: (i) a delay model and effective
discount for consequence learning, (ii) replay-time alignment of an emitted
consequence to an earlier action when a delay label is available, (iii) an ICN
that estimates an action-centred contrast, (iv) a state-conditioned and bounded
safety weight, and (v) separate reward and consequence critics. The
implementation also includes an uncertainty/OOD gate that adds bounded
conservative margins when the ICN is uncertain or the state is outside its
observed distribution.

The scope is deliberately limited. The simulator provides interventional SCM
labels, so the causal component tests whether an action-level contrast is
useful when such supervision is available. It does not show that an SCM can
be recovered from observational data. The state-conditioned multiplier is an
adaptive parameterization and training heuristic; it is not claimed to
strictly dominate a scalar multiplier in all CMDPs. The delay analysis
concerns the fixed-kernel tabular update represented by the code, not arbitrary
neural approximation or changing environments.

The contributions are: an explicit delayed-consequence pipeline with replay
alignment; an action-centred, SCM-supervised ICN with uncertainty estimation;
a dual-critic and adaptive safety-selection design; and an implementation-level
evaluation protocol with component ablations, attribution diagnostics, and a
visual delayed-cost case study. The code also exposes the supervision masks,
source-action indices, uncertainty margins, and diagnostic logs needed to
audit these mechanisms rather than treating them as an opaque policy network.

\section{Background and Related Work}
CMDPs define a reward objective together with a constraint budget
\citep{altman1999}. CPO uses trust-region policy updates to address constrained
policy optimization \citep{achiam2017}. Other constrained methods commonly use
Lagrangian or primal--dual updates. CCPL addresses a different issue: how an
observed consequence is temporally assigned and how action-specific risk is
separated from risk already present in the state or consequence pipeline.

The delayed-observation problem is related to partially observable decision
making, where histories or belief states summarize information not present in
the current observation \citep{kaelbling1998}. In CCPL, $h_t$ is a finite
history representation rather than a proof that the hidden pipeline state has
been reconstructed. The physical dynamics may remain Markov in an augmented
state containing pending events.

The causal notation follows structural causal models and intervention-based
reasoning \citep{pearl2009,peters2017}. The important distinction is between a
conditional observational contrast and an interventional estimand. CCPL uses
the latter only in the controlled simulator, where counterfactual labels are
generated by known structural equations. This makes the benchmark useful for
isolating attribution, but it also creates a privileged-information limitation
that is evaluated separately from the RL policy.

\section{Problem Formulation}
Let a finite-horizon CMDP have state $s_t\in\mathcal{S}$, discrete action
$a_t\in\mathcal{A}$, transition kernel $P$, reward $r_t$, non-negative
consequence $c_t$, discount $\gamma\in(0,1)$, horizon $T$, and budget $d$.
For policy $\pi$,
\begin{equation}
J_r(\pi)=\E_\pi\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t r_t\right],\qquad
J_c(\pi)=\E_\pi\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t c_t\right].
\end{equation}
The target problem is $\max_\pi J_r(\pi)$ subject to $J_c(\pi)\le d$.

\subsection{Delayed consequence observation}
Let $x_t$ denote physical state and $z_t$ pending consequence state. A useful
abstraction is
\begin{align}
x_{t+1}&\sim P(\cdot\mid x_t,a_t),\\
z_{t+1}&=F(z_t,x_t,a_t,\xi_t),\\
c_t&=G(z_t,\eta_t),
\end{align}
where $\xi_t,\eta_t$ are exogenous disturbances. The augmented state
$(x_t,z_t)$ is Markov when $z_t$ is observed. When it is hidden, the learner
uses a history $h_t$ as a finite summary. The issue is not that physical
dynamics violate the Markov property; it is that the emitted consequence is
not necessarily caused by the action active when it is observed.

Let $\tau_t\in\{0,\ldots,K\}$ denote the delay associated with a consequence
and $p(k\mid h_t)$ its learned conditional distribution. CCPL uses
\begin{equation}
\bar\gamma(h_t)=\sum_{k=0}^{K}p(k\mid h_t)\gamma^k.
\label{eq:gammaeff}
\end{equation}
This quantity is a delay weight, not an estimate of causal responsibility by
itself. Responsibility comes from the aligned replay label or SCM contrast.

\subsection{Replay alignment and delay learning}
During interaction, the agent stores $(s_t,a_t,r_t,c_t,s_{t+1})$, hidden
states, and the finite causal history. When the environment exposes
\texttt{actual\_tau}, CCPL walks backward by that offset and writes the emitted
consequence into the earlier transition using
\texttt{set\_aligned\_consequence}.
Transitions without a valid delay label are not treated as aligned causal
supervision. The delay network is trained with weighted categorical
cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{delay}}(\phi)=
-\frac{1}{\sum_i w_i}\sum_iw_i\log p_\phi(\tau_i\mid h_i),
\qquad w_i\ge0.
\end{equation}
If too few observed delay labels exist, the implementation uses a bounded
online prior for warm-start updates; this is a numerical fallback, not a
claim that the prior is correct.

\subsection{Implementation-aligned critic targets}
The reward critic keeps the ordinary one-step target:
\begin{equation}
y^r_t=r_t+\gamma(1-d_t)Q_r^-(s_{t+1},a_{t+1}).
\label{eq:reward-target}
\end{equation}
The consequence critic uses the positive clipped attributed consequence:
\begin{equation}
y^c_t=\bar\gamma(h_t)[\Delta C_t]_+ +
\gamma(1-d_t)Q_c^-(s_{t+1},a_{t+1}).
\label{eq:qc}
\end{equation}
The multiplier does not appear in either critic target. It enters when actions
are scored. This separation makes the reward and consequence estimates
independently inspectable as the safety weight changes.

\subsection{Action-centred consequence attribution}
For a reference action $a_0$, the simulator supplies an interventional
contrast of the form
\begin{equation}
\Delta C(s,a,h)=C^{\mathrm{do}(a)}(s,h)-
C^{\mathrm{do}(a_0)}(s,h).
\label{eq:contrast}
\end{equation}
The ICN predicts a total consequence and a same-state action baseline,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta C}_\psi(s,a,h)=
f_{\mathrm{total},\psi}(s,a,h)-f_{\mathrm{base},\psi}(s,h).
\end{equation}
The state is retained in both heads; the baseline omits the current action so
that it represents action-marginal consequence at the same state and history.
For valid labels, the weighted objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ICN}}(\psi)=
\frac{1}{\sum_iw_i}\sum_{i=1}^{B}w_i
\left(\widehat{\Delta C}_\psi(s_i,a_i,h_i)-\Delta C_i\right)^2.
\end{equation}
The implementation also trains a positive uncertainty scale $\sigma_\psi$
with a Laplace-style error objective. The uncertainty is used by the penalty
gate to add a bounded margin; it never turns an uncertain estimate into a
smaller safety penalty.

\subsection{Adaptive safety selection}
The multiplier is bounded by
\begin{equation}
\lambda_\phi(s)=\lambda_{\max}\,\sigma(f_\phi(s)),
\qquad 0\le\lambda_\phi(s)\le\lambda_{\max}.
\end{equation}
The implementation trains its target using global episode violation, local
attributed consequence, and recent hit frequency, with risk-weighted replay
updates and a warm-up scale. These terms are an adaptive control heuristic for
the single aggregate constraint used by the repository, not a closed-form
statewise dual optimum.

At action selection, the policy combines reward value, the consequence critic,
and the ICN estimate:
\begin{equation}
a_t=\arg\max_a\left\{
Q_r(s_t,a)-\Pi_{\mathrm{ICN}}(s_t,a,h_t)-
\lambda_\phi(s_t)\Pi_{Q_c}(s_t,a)\right\}.
\end{equation}
Both penalty terms are clipped. The implementation gradually shifts from the
direct ICN penalty toward the learned consequence critic as updates accumulate.
The HallucinationGate adds bounded uncertainty and OOD margins and records
calibration statistics for later audit.

\section{Formal Analysis}
The following result is intentionally about the fixed-kernel tabular map
represented by Equation~\eqref{eq:qc}. It does not analyze the nonlinear
optimizer, the learned history encoder, or online SCM calibration.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-kernel contraction of the consequence critic]
Let $P$, $p(\tau\mid h)$, and $\Delta C$ be fixed. Assume bounded
$\Delta C$ and $0<\gamma<1$. Define
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal{T}_cQ)(s,a)=\bar\gamma(h)[\Delta C(s,a,h)]_+
 +\gamma\E_{s'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,a)}[\max_b Q(s',b)].
\label{eq:tabular_operator}
\end{equation}
Then $\mathcal{T}_c$ is a contraction on bounded action-value functions under
the sup norm, with modulus at most $\gamma$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any $Q_1,Q_2$, the consequence terms in
Equation~\eqref{eq:tabular_operator} are identical and cancel. For every
next state $s'$,
\begin{equation}
\left|\max_bQ_1(s',b)-\max_bQ_2(s',b)\right|
\le \max_b|Q_1(s',b)-Q_2(s',b)|
\le \|Q_1-Q_2\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
Taking the expectation under the probability kernel $P$ and multiplying by
$\gamma$ gives
\begin{align}
\left|(\mathcal{T}_cQ_1)(s,a)-(\mathcal{T}_cQ_2)(s,a)\right|
&\le \gamma\|Q_1-Q_2\|_\infty.
\end{align}
Taking the supremum over $(s,a)$ proves the result. Since the space of
bounded functions with the sup norm is complete, the fixed-kernel map has a
unique fixed point and repeated exact application of this map converges to it.
The conclusion applies to the fixed-kernel tabular map only; it does not
establish convergence of the learned neural update or correctness of the
learned delay distribution.
\end{proof}

The reward critic in the implementation uses the ordinary one-step target in
Equation~\eqref{eq:reward-target}; the delay weight enters the consequence
signal in Equation~\eqref{eq:qc}. Thus the theorem is a contraction result for
the actual fixed-kernel consequence-critic target, not a claim that a learned
delay model recovers the true delayed return.

\begin{proposition}[Bounds on the learned delay weight]
If $p(k\mid h)$ is a probability distribution on $\{0,\ldots,K\}$, then
$\gamma^K\le\bar\gamma(h)\le1$. If there is a uniform positive minimum
delay $k_{\min}\ge1$, then $\bar\gamma(h)\le\gamma^{k_{\min}}<1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The values $\gamma^k$ lie in $[\gamma^K,1]$ and
$\bar\gamma(h)$ is their convex combination. Under the support restriction,
every nonzero term is at most $\gamma^{k_{\min}}$.
\end{proof}

The proposition is a bound on the weighting signal. It does not imply that
an arbitrary unknown delay process satisfies a positive-minimum-delay
assumption, and the contraction theorem uses the ordinary one-step modulus
$\gamma$ because that is what the implementation bootstraps.

\subsection{Training procedure}
Each episode resets the policy and causal histories, observes transitions, and
updates the aligned replay record when delayed feedback identifies a previous
source action. Once a batch is available, the update order is: encode stored
histories; update the ICN on valid SCM/aligned labels; periodically recalibrate
against SCM labels; update the delay model; compute $y^r_t$ and $y^c_t$; update
the two critics; update the multiplier; update replay priorities; and softly
update target networks. This order keeps history-dependent labels attached to
the transition that generated them.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Synthetic benchmark}
The primary benchmark is a six-dimensional continuous-state, five-action
synthetic CMDP. The repository contains standard, noisy, shifted, randomized,
deceptive-reward, and resource-collapse variants, together with held-out
adversarial environments for transfer. The action space is discrete, and the
environments expose consequence metadata needed for controlled diagnostics.
The benchmark is useful for mechanism isolation because the structural
equations and causal labels are available to the evaluator.

The archived result snapshot reports six environments and three seeds. We use
that snapshot exactly as supplied and do not combine it with later Kaggle or
Safety Gymnasium runs. Reward, consequence return, and CSR retain their
original definitions; CSR is the percentage of evaluation episodes whose
consequence return is within the configured budget.

\subsection{Baselines and ablations}
The archived comparison includes PPO, A2C, DQN, CPO-FO, RCPO, PID-Lag,
SAC-Lag, and CCPL-Base. CPO-FO is the repository's first-order constrained
policy-optimization baseline and is not presented as an exact natural-gradient
implementation of CPO. CCPL-Base removes the four principal CCPL directions.
The component ablations remove one direction at a time while preserving the
rest of the architecture. No statistical significance value is reported here
unless the corresponding replicate-level artifact is available.

\subsection{Practical case study: SafeRoute}
SafeRoute is a separate synthetic grid-navigation environment. Its 12-element
observation contains normalized agent and goal coordinates, remaining horizon,
current and adjacent hazard indicators, normalized Manhattan distance, and the
most recently queued cost. The five actions are up, down, left, right, and
wait. For candidate cell $q_t$, the immediate cost is
\begin{equation}
\tilde c_t=\mathbf{1}\{q_t\text{ is hazardous}\}+
0.15\mathbf{1}\{q_t\text{ is outside the grid}\}.
\end{equation}
For delay $d>0$, the environment emits the oldest queued cost and appends
$\tilde c_t$; pending costs are flushed at termination. The route reward is
progress-shaped with a terminal goal bonus and the configured budget is $3$.
The figures are qualitative visual artifacts because a matching multi-seed
summary was not preserved with the selected images.

\subsection{Attribution and delay diagnostics}
The E3 diagnostic measures agreement with SCM reference labels. It should be
read as supervised approximation quality, not evidence of causal identification.
In the reported run, final ICN performance is MAE $0.03778$, correlation
$0.9252$, and sign agreement $0.9160$ after 100 training episodes. The
positive mean reference contrast is associated with the `FULL` action in the
synthetic action set. These results indicate that the ICN learns the supplied
simulator contrast under this protocol.

The E4 diagnostic exposes a limitation. With an observed constant delay of
$10$, the predicted delay mean is approximately $5.26$ and calibration MAE is
approximately $4.742$. The delay model should therefore not be described as
accurately recovering the observed delay in that run. Its value in the
implementation is a learned weighting signal, and its calibration must be
reported rather than assumed.

\section{Limitations and Discussion}
The strongest limitation is the ICN supervision regime. The simulator exposes
the SCM and supplies interventional labels, which isolates the attribution
question but gives the method information unavailable in many real systems.
An observational-data version would require assumptions or experimental
interventions sufficient to identify the relevant effect. Even with correct
labels, the implemented contrast is action-level and does not solve unique
responsibility when several actions jointly contribute to one violation.

The benchmark is also narrow. Its six environments are variants of one
synthetic CMDP, and SafeRoute is a separate synthetic demonstration. The
reported archived tables use few seeds, and later experiments are not merged
without their raw configurations and replicate files. Results should therefore
be interpreted as evidence for a mechanism under controlled conditions, not as
state-of-the-art evidence across constrained RL or real-world safety.

There are implementation limitations as well. The history encoder is a
deterministic recency-weighted summary, not an end-to-end trained recurrent
causal representation. The delay prior is used during warm start when labels
are sparse. Uncertainty and OOD margins are bounded heuristics. The scalar
class is representable by the state-conditioned multiplier, but that fact does
not imply a finite-sample performance guarantee. Finally, bounded multiplier
updates and target networks stabilize numerical behavior but do not certify
convergence of the complete nonlinear system.

The practical contribution is consequently best stated as a research
implementation and evaluation framework for delayed, attributed safety
consequences. Its next tests should use noisy or misspecified SCM labels,
matched seeds across all baselines, stronger contemporary baselines, and
domain-specific validation before any deployment claim.

\section{Conclusion}
CCPL makes delayed consequence attribution an explicit part of constrained RL
training. Its implementation aligns delayed observations when labels permit,
learns an action-centred consequence contrast under a controlled SCM, uses
separate reward and consequence critics, and adds bounded uncertainty-aware
safety margins. The archived benchmark and ablation support the usefulness of
this combination in the tested synthetic setting. They do not establish
general causal discovery, universal superiority, or certified safety.

\section{Complete Update and Data Flow}
For each transition, the replay record contains the current state $s_t$,
action $a_t$, reward $r_t$, emitted consequence $c_t$, next state $s_{t+1}$,
done flag, current and next hidden states, a fixed-length history, aligned
consequence, and validity flags. The aligned flag is set only when the
environment identifies a valid source delay. The SCM flag identifies records
for which simulator labels can be generated.

The online sequence is: reset hidden state and causal history; compute
all-action ICN contrasts and uncertainty; compute $\bar\gamma(h_t)$ and
$\lambda_\phi(s_t)$; score and execute an action; push the transition; align
the emitted consequence when \texttt{actual\_tau} is valid; sample prioritized replay;
and update the ICN, delay network, critics, multiplier, priorities, and target
networks in that order.

The ICN pretraining uses two stages. The baseline head is fitted to same-state
baseline targets, then the total head is fitted to per-action total
consequences while the uncertainty head is trained jointly. Pretraining
diagnostics are held-out agreement measures and are not proof certificates.

\section{Derivations and Assumptions}
\subsection{Effective delay weight}
Since $0<\gamma<1$ and $0\le k\le K$, $\gamma^k\in[\gamma^K,1]$.
Because $p(k\mid h)$ is non-negative and sums to one,
\begin{equation}
\gamma^K\le\bar\gamma(h)\le1.
\end{equation}
If a known support condition gives $p(k\mid h)=0$ for $k<k_{\min}$, with
$k_{\min}\ge1$, then $\bar\gamma(h)\le\gamma^{k_{\min}}<1$. This does not
say that arbitrary unknown stochastic delays satisfy the support condition.

\subsection{Fixed-kernel contraction calculation}
For fixed bounded $r$, $\lambda$, and $\Delta C$, the tabular update matching
the implementation is
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal{T}Q)(s,a)=r(s,a)-\lambda(s)\bar\gamma(h)[\Delta C(s,a,h)]_+
 +\gamma\E_{s'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,a)}[\max_bQ(s',b)].
\end{equation}
For any bounded functions $Q,Q'$, the additive reward and penalty cancel:
\begin{align}
\left|(\mathcal{T}Q)-(\mathcal{T}Q')\right|
&\le\gamma\E_{s'\sim P}\left[
\left|\max_bQ(s',b)-\max_bQ'(s',b)\right|\right]\\
&\le\gamma\|Q-Q'\|_\infty.
\end{align}
The second inequality follows from
$|\max_bu_b-\max_bv_b|\le\max_b|u_b-v_b|$. Thus the fixed-kernel tabular
map has modulus at most $\gamma<1$. This calculation does not imply
convergence for neural function approximation, changing delay kernels, or
online SCM estimation.

\subsection{ICN objective and uncertainty}
The ICN total head uses $(s,a,h)$ while the baseline head uses $(s,h)$. For
valid weights, the normalized objective is well-defined when $\sum_iw_i>0$.
Samples with invalid SCM and alignment masks receive zero causal-supervision
weight. The uncertainty head produces $\sigma_i>0$ through a softplus map and
uses
\begin{equation}
\ell_i^{\mathrm{Laplace}}=\log(\sigma_i+\varepsilon)+
\frac{|C_i-\widehat C_i|}{\sigma_i+\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
The gate clips positive consequence estimates and adds a bounded uncertainty
margin. These operations are engineering safeguards, not coverage guarantees.

\section{Environment and Metric Details}
The six synthetic benchmark variants modify consequence noise, state shifts,
delay structure, reward/consequence interaction, and resource pressure while
retaining the common five-action interface. The adversarial variants are held
out for transfer experiments. All claims in the main tables refer only to the
archived snapshot and its stated seed count.

For evaluation episodes $\mathcal{D}$ and budget $d$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CSR}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{D}}
\mathbf{1}\{J_c^{(e)}\le d\}.
\end{equation}
This episode-level metric differs from a guarantee that every future
trajectory satisfies the constraint.

\section{Artifact and Reproducibility Notes}
The repository provides the installable `ccpl` package, synthetic benchmark
runner, ablation builders, environment adapters, SafeRoute, and the Safety
Audit companion tool. Airspace Guardian and Neurocity are visual demonstrations
using synthetic worlds; they are not flight-control or clinical systems. The
Safety Audit tool reports robustness for specified seeds, environments, delays,
and thresholds; its output is not a universal safety certificate.

The archived tables should be reproduced only from the source revision and
configuration that generated the supplied snapshot. The two SafeRoute images
in the main text are copied from the recorded visualization suite and carry no
new aggregate metrics.

\section{Failure Analysis}
The main failure modes are invalid or missing delay labels; poorly calibrated
delay predictions; ICN disagreement with SCM labels for rare actions; multiple
actions jointly contributing to one consequence; and distribution shift beyond
the states used to calibrate the uncertainty/OOD gate. These failures motivate
the diagnostics and conservative scope of the paper.

\section{Optional Ethics Statement}
All reported controlled experiments use synthetic environments and do not
involve human subjects, personal data, or live infrastructure. The released
domain demonstrations are simulations and should not be connected directly to
operational systems. A policy satisfying a simulated budget is not certified
for physical deployment. Any real deployment would require domain-specific
hazard analysis, independent validation, monitoring, fail-safe controls, and
appropriate regulatory review.
