# Mathematical Scope

This document states the assumptions used by the current implementation. It
does not replace the formal definitions or proofs in the paper.

## Delayed Consequences

Let (h_t) be the information available at time (t), and let the delay
(\tau_t\) take values in (\{0,\ldots,K\}\). For a conditional delay law
(p(\tau\mid h_t)), CCPL uses

\[
\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}(h_t)
  = \mathbb{E}[\gamma^{\tau_t}\mid h_t]
  = \sum_{\tau=0}^{K}p(\tau\mid h_t)\gamma^\tau.
\]

For (0<\gamma<1),

\[
\gamma^K \leq \gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}(h_t) \leq 1.
\]

The upper bound equals one whenever zero delay has positive probability.
Therefore an arbitrary unknown stochastic delay does not establish a
contraction modulus strictly below one. Such a result requires a positive
minimum delay, for example (p(\tau\mid h)=0) for all
(\tau<\tau_{\min}) with (\tau_{\min}\geq 1), or another assumption that
supplies an equivalent uniform bound.

The implementation-level bounds are checked by
`ccpl_theory.effective_discount_bounds`. This check does not infer the delay
law or prove that an environment satisfies the theorem assumptions.

## Constraint Objective

For policy (\pi_\theta), reward return (J_r(\theta)), consequence return
(J_c(\theta)), and budget (d), the constrained problem is

\[
\max_\theta J_r(\theta)
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
J_c(\theta)\leq d.
\]

The Lagrangian is

\[
\mathcal{L}(\theta,\lambda)
  = J_r(\theta)-\lambda\bigl(J_c(\theta)-d\bigr),
\qquad \lambda\geq 0.
\]

CCPL allows the multiplier to depend on state, (\lambda=\lambda_\phi(s)).
This changes the policy parameterization and does not imply universal
dominance over a scalar multiplier for every CMDP, policy class, or data
distribution.

## Causal Attribution

Synthetic environments expose labels generated by their programmed structural
causal model. For an action (a), a label may be written as

\[
Y^{\mathrm{do}(a)}=f_a(S,U),
\]

where (S) is the observed state and (U) contains exogenous variables. The
Consequence Net is trained against these interventional labels when available.
Agreement with (Y^{\mathrm{do}(a)}) tests the implementation against the
synthetic SCM; it is not causal identification from observational data and is
not a claim about unlabelled external tasks.

## Separate Value Functions

Reward and consequence critics are represented separately:

\[
Q_r(s,a)\quad\text{and}\quad Q_c(s,a).
\]

The policy objective combines their estimates, but the critic targets remain
separate. Updates to (\lambda_\phi(s)) therefore do not redefine either
Bellman target.
