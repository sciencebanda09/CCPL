# CCPL Theorems and Derivations

This document gives the mathematical derivation of the operators implemented
in CCPL. It separates proved statements from assumptions, diagnostics, and
empirical hypotheses. It must be read together with
[MATHEMATICAL_SPEC.md](MATHEMATICAL_SPEC.md) and the paper.

All displayed equations use GitHub's supported double-dollar math blocks and
all inline expressions use single-dollar math delimiters. View this file on
the rendered GitHub page rather than the raw-file view.

## 1. Setting and notation

Let

$$
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,c,\gamma)
$$

be a discounted constrained Markov decision process. The state is
$s_t\in\mathcal{S}$, the discrete action is $a_t\in\mathcal{A}$, the reward is
$r_t$, and the non-negative consequence or safety cost is $c_t$. The discount
satisfies $0\leq\gamma<1$.

The policy is $\pi_\theta(a\mid s)$ and the two discounted returns are

$$
J_r(\pi)
  =\mathbb{E}_\pi\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^t r_t\right],
\qquad
J_c(\pi)
  =\mathbb{E}_\pi\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^t c_t\right].
$$

For a budget $d$, the constrained objective is

$$
\max_{\pi} J_r(\pi)
\quad\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad
J_c(\pi)\leq d.
$$

CCPL uses a history representation $h_t$ generated by the recurrent state
encoder. The history is used to predict delay and consequence attribution; it
does not make a non-Markov environment Markov without additional assumptions.

## 2. Delayed consequences

Suppose the consequence caused by an action at time $t$ is observed after a
random delay $\tau_t\in\{0,\ldots,K\}$. Conditional on $h_t$, let

$$
p_k(h_t)=\Pr(\tau_t=k\mid h_t),
\qquad
\sum_{k=0}^{K}p_k(h_t)=1,
\qquad
p_k(h_t)\geq 0.
$$

The delay model estimates the vector
$p(h_t)=(p_0(h_t),\ldots,p_K(h_t))$ with a categorical output. The associated
effective discount is

$$
\bar{\gamma}(h_t)
  =\mathbb{E}\left[\gamma^{\tau_t}\mid h_t\right]
  =\sum_{k=0}^{K}p_k(h_t)\gamma^k.
$$

### Proposition 1: support bounds

Under the probability-simplex assumptions above,

$$
\gamma^K\leq\bar{\gamma}(h_t)\leq 1.
$$

### Proof

Since $0\leq\gamma<1$, the sequence
$1,\gamma,\ldots,\gamma^K$ is decreasing. Therefore, for every
$k\in\{0,\ldots,K\}$,

$$
\gamma^K\leq\gamma^k\leq 1.
$$

Multiplying by $p_k(h_t)\geq 0$ and summing gives

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{K}p_k(h_t)\gamma^K
\leq
\sum_{k=0}^{K}p_k(h_t)\gamma^k
\leq
\sum_{k=0}^{K}p_k(h_t).
$$

Using $\sum_k p_k(h_t)=1$ yields the result. $\square$

### Positive-minimum-delay corollary

Assume there is a uniform $\tau_{\min}\geq 1$ such that

$$
p_k(h)=0
\quad\mathrm{for\ all}\ k<\tau_{\min}
\quad\mathrm{and\ all\ admissible}\ h.
$$

Then

$$
\bar{\gamma}(h)
  \leq \gamma^{\tau_{\min}}
  <1.
$$

This is the condition that supplies a strict upper bound below one. An
arbitrary unknown stochastic delay does not supply this condition. In
particular, if $p_0(h)>0$ is possible, then $\bar{\gamma}(h)$ can approach or
equal one.

## 3. Bellman operators

For a fixed policy, the ordinary reward Bellman operator is

$$
(\mathcal{T}_r Q)(s,a)
  =\mathbb{E}\left[
    r_t+\gamma Q(s_{t+1},a_{t+1})
    \mid s_t=s,a_t=a
  \right],
$$

where $a_{t+1}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s_{t+1})$. The optimal one-step version
replaces the next-policy value by $\max_{a'}Q(s_{t+1},a')$.

For bounded functions $Q_1,Q_2$ and the sup norm,

$$
\left\|\mathcal{T}_rQ_1-\mathcal{T}_rQ_2\right\|_\infty
\leq
\gamma\left\|Q_1-Q_2\right\|_\infty.
$$

Therefore the ordinary reward Bellman operator is a $\gamma$-contraction
whenever $0\leq\gamma<1$ and expected rewards are bounded.

### Implemented target

Delayed observation does not change the temporal distance from $s_t$ to
$s_{t+1}$. The reward bootstrap remains

$$
y^r_t
  =r_t+\gamma(1-\mathrm{done}_t)
    Q_r^{\mathrm{target}}(s_{t+1},a_{t+1}).
$$

The learned delay factor is applied to the attributed consequence term:

$$
y^{\mathrm{policy}}_t
  =r_t+\gamma(1-\mathrm{done}_t)
    Q_r^{\mathrm{target}}(s_{t+1},a_{t+1})
   -\lambda(s_t)\,\bar{\gamma}(h_t)\,
    \Delta C(s_t,a_t,h_t).
$$

The implementation clips the attributed consequence to its configured
non-negative range before applying the penalty. The penalty scale is a
configuration parameter and must be reported with an experiment.

## 4. Consequence attribution

Let $C^{\mathrm{do}(a)}(s,u)$ be the consequence generated by intervening on
the action and let $a_0$ be a reference action. The action-level causal
contrast is

$$
\Delta C(s,a,u)
  =C^{\mathrm{do}(a)}(s,u)
   -C^{\mathrm{do}(a_0)}(s,u).
$$

The synthetic structural causal model supplies labels for this quantity. The
Consequence Net, with parameters $\psi$, predicts

$$
\widehat{\Delta C}_\psi(s,a,h).
$$

With valid interventional labels, the supervised objective has the form

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ICN}}(\psi)
  &=
  \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{B} w_i}
  \sum_{i=1}^{B}
  w_i
  \left(
    \widehat{\Delta C}_\psi(s_i,a_i,h_i)-\Delta C_i
  \right)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

where $w_i\geq 0$ are replay or confidence weights. Samples without a valid
causal label are not treated as causal supervision.

This objective evaluates prediction against a known SCM. It does not identify
$C^{\mathrm{do}(a)}$ from observational data alone.

## 5. Separate reward and consequence critics

CCPL maintains separate value functions,

$$
Q_r(s,a;\omega_r)
\quad\text{and}\quad
Q_c(s,a;\omega_c).
$$

Their one-step targets have separate definitions:

$$
y^r_t
  =r_t+\gamma(1-\mathrm{done}_t)
    Q_r^{\mathrm{target}}(s_{t+1},a_{t+1}),
$$

and

$$
y^c_t
  =c_t+\gamma(1-\mathrm{done}_t)
    Q_c^{\mathrm{target}}(s_{t+1},a_{t+1}).
$$

The critic losses are

$$
\mathcal{L}_r(\omega_r)
  =\mathbb{E}\left[(Q_r(s_t,a_t;\omega_r)-y^r_t)^2\right],
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}_c(\omega_c)
  =\mathbb{E}\left[(Q_c(s_t,a_t;\omega_c)-y^c_t)^2\right].
$$

The multiplier is used in the policy objective, not inserted into the
definition of either critic target. This removes the direct dependence of the
critic targets on $\lambda$.

## 6. Lagrangian policy objective

For a scalar multiplier, the CMDP Lagrangian is

$$
\mathcal{L}(\pi,\lambda)
  =J_r(\pi)-\lambda\bigl(J_c(\pi)-d\bigr),
\qquad \lambda\geq 0.
$$

The corresponding action-selection score is

$$
Q_{\mathcal{L}}(s,a)
  =Q_r(s,a)-\lambda Q_c(s,a).
$$

CCPL replaces the scalar with a bounded state-conditioned multiplier

$$
\lambda_\phi(s)
  =\lambda_{\max}\sigma(f_\phi(s)),
\qquad
0\leq\lambda_\phi(s)\leq\lambda_{\max},
$$

where $\sigma(x)=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}$. The policy score becomes

$$
Q_{\mathrm{CCPL}}(s,a)
  =Q_r(s,a)-\lambda_\phi(s)Q_c(s,a).
$$

For an entropy-regularized discrete policy, the objective has the form

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{J}_{\pi}
  &=
  \mathbb{E}_{s}\left[
    \sum_a \pi_\theta(a\mid s)
    \left(
      \alpha\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)
      -Q_r(s,a)
      +\lambda_\phi(s)Q_c(s,a)
    \right)
  \right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The implementation uses bounded numerical updates for $\lambda_\phi$. A
bounded multiplier is a numerical invariant, not a convergence proof.

## 7. Scope of the results

The following statements follow from the derivations above:

1. The delay-factor support bound
   $\gamma^K\leq\bar{\gamma}(h)\leq 1$ follows from the probability simplex.
2. A strict delay-factor bound below one follows when a positive minimum delay
   is assumed.
3. The ordinary one-step reward Bellman operator has modulus $\gamma$ under
   the standard boundedness assumptions.
4. Separate critics have separate target definitions.

The following statements are not established by these equations alone:

1. Unknown stochastic delays automatically imply contraction.
2. A state-conditioned multiplier universally dominates a scalar multiplier.
3. Lower ICN prediction error proves causal identification in an external
   environment.
4. A finite or bounded multiplier log proves stochastic-approximation
   convergence.
5. Any trained CCPL policy is safe for deployment without an independent
   action validator and system-level safety case.

The repository theory utilities therefore report numerical diagnostics and
explicitly label empirical or unevaluated conditions; they do not claim to
machine-prove convergence, dominance, causal identification, or transfer.
